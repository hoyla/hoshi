Harbour authority paid consultant twice for the same report

The Westmere Harbour Authority paid a consultancy 186,000 pounds for a dredging study in March 2019, and then paid the same firm 172,500 pounds for a second study fourteen months later. The two reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 of the 48 pages are the same, including a typing error on page 12.

Neither payment was put out to tender. The authority's own rules require three written quotes for any contract worth more than 50,000 pounds. Its chief executive, Margaret Oduya, told us the rules were not broken because the work was "urgent and specialised".

That is not what the board was told. Minutes of a meeting held on 9 May 2020 record that the second study was described as "a fresh assessment by a new team". No new team existed. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had left the firm before the second report was delivered.

The consultancy, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portbury. Its only director is Daniel Reyes, who sat on the harbour authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not reply to five emails and two letters. When we reached him by telephone he said he had nothing to add and ended the call.

A former finance officer at the authority, who asked not to be named, said she raised concerns in writing three times. "I was told it had been approved at the top and that I should process the invoice," she said. She was never given a reason. Her post was made redundant four months later, although the authority says the two events are unrelated.

The dredging itself has still not been done. Silt in the main channel is now 1.8 metres deeper than the safe limit, according to a survey carried out for the port's insurers in January. Two cargo vessels have been turned away this year. A third ran aground in February and had to wait eleven hours for the tide.

Local businesses say they are paying the price. The fish market has lost about a fifth of its trade since 2021. A ferry operator cancelled its summer service to the islands, and the owner of the boatyard told us he has laid off six of his fourteen workers.

The authority receives 2.4 million pounds a year in public funding. It has not published audited accounts since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an inquiry was opened last autumn but would not say when it will report, or whether the two payments are part of it.

In a written statement, the authority said it "takes its duties seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorised". It did not answer our questions about why the reports are the same, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr Reyes declared an interest. It also declined to release the invoices.

We have asked the transport ministry whether it knew about the payments before it renewed the authority's grant. A spokesperson said the ministry does not comment on individual cases. The local member of parliament, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the board to resign.
