The port authority paid a consultant twice for the same report.

The Port Authority of Westmere paid a consultant 186,000 pounds sterling for a dredging study in March 2019, and then paid the same company 172,500 pounds sterling for a second study, fourteen months later. The two reports are almost identical. We compare them line by line and discover that 41 of the 48 pages are equal, including a typographic error on page 12.

None of the payments were submitted to public competition. The Authority's own rules require three written budgets for any contract exceeding £50,000. His executive director, Margaret Oduya, told us that the rules had not been breached because the work was "urgent and specialized."

That's not what was communicated to the council. The minutes of a meeting held on 9 May 2020 indicate that the second study was described as "a new evaluation by a new team." There was no new team. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had left the company before the second report was given.

The consultant, Tidewell Associates, is registered in a residential address in Portbury. Its only director is Daniel Reyes, who was on the finance committee of the port authority until 2017. He didn't answer five e-mails and two letters. When we contacted him on the phone, he said he had nothing to add and hung up on.

A former government finance officer, who asked not to be appointed, said she expressed her concerns in writing three times. "I was told that it had been approved by the management and that I should process the invoice," he said. He was never given a reason. His post was removed four months later, although the authority claims that the two events are not related.

The dredging work itself has not yet been done. According to a study carried out for the port insurers in January, the sediment in the main channel is now 1.8 meters deeper than is safe. Two cargo ships have been rejected this year. A third slammed in February and had to wait eleven hours to the tide.

Local companies say they're paying the price. The fish market has lost approximately one fifth of its activity since 2021. A ferry operator cancelled his summer service to the islands, and the shipyard owner told us that he has fired six of his fourteen employees.

The authority receives £2.4 million a year in public funds. It has not published audited accounts since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was opened last fall, but did not mean when the report will be published, or whether the two payments are part of it.

In a written statement, the authority stated that "it takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments were duly authorized." He did not answer our questions about why the reports are equal, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes declared a conflict of interest. He also refused to disclose the invoices.

We asked the Ministry of Transport if he was aware of the payments before renewing the grant from the authority. One speaker said the ministry does not comment on individual cases. The local deputy, Hannah Whitlock, has asked the council to resign.
