The port organization paid twice as much for the same report.

Wester’s port agency paid £186,000 in March 2019 to a consulting company for a Leipzig-related study, and then received the same company fourteen months later, £17,500 for another study. The two reports are almost identical. We compared them to the line and found that 41 out of 48 pages are the same, including a typographic error on page 12.

None of these payments were made through the tender. The internal rules of the organization require three written proposals for each contract worth more than £50,000. Margaret Odoya, CEO of the organization, told us that the rules were not violated because it was “expert and specialized.”

It was not what was told to the board. The meeting of a meeting held on May 9, 2020 shows that the second study was described as “recommission by a new team.” There was no new team. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them was separated from the company before the second report was presented.

The consulting company, called “Tole Associates,” is registered in a residential address in Portbury. The company’s only manager is Daniel Re, who has been a member of the Port Organization’s Financial Committee by 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we called him a phone, he said he had nothing to add and interrupted the call.

One of the former financial staff of the organization, who did not want to be identified, said he had raised concerns three times in writing. “I was told that this was confirmed at higher levels and I had to process the invoice.” There was no specific reason for him. Four months later, his job was removed, although the organization claims that the two events have nothing to do with each other.

Operational has not yet been done. According to a review conducted for insurance companies, the amount of sediments in the main channel is now 1.8 meters deeper than the limit. Two ships have been banned from entering this port this year. Another ship sat in the water in February and had to wait 11 hours for the tide.

Local business owners say they are paying the price. The fish market has lost about a fifth of its trade volume since 2021. A marine transport company has cancelled its summer service to the islands, and the owner of a shipyard told us that he had fired six of his fourteen workers.

The organization receives £2.4 million annually from public funding. Since 2020, the organization has not published its audited financial statements. The Regional Inspector confirmed that a study started in the past autumn, but did not say anything since it was presented, or whether the two payments were part of it.

In a written statement, the organization declared that “it takes its position seriously” and “all payments are properly confirmed.” The organization did not respond to our questions about why these reports were the same, who confirmed the second payment, or whether Mr. Rees has declared certain interests. He also refused to publish the invoices.

We asked the Ministry of Transport whether they were aware of these payments before extending funding to the organization. A spokesman for the ministry said the ministry did not comment on individual cases. The local Parliament Representative Hanna Witlock calls for the resignation of the board.
