The Port Authority paid a consultancy twice for the same report.

The Westmere Port Authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 for a dredging study in March 2019 and paid the same firm £172,500 for a second study fourteen months later. The two reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages are identical, including a typo on page 12.

Neither payment was identified by a tendering procedure. The agency’s own rules require three written offers for each order worth more than £50,000. Managing Director Margaret Oduya told us that the rules were not violated because the work was "urgent and specialized".

However, this was not communicated to the board. The minutes of a sitting of the 9th May 2020 show that the second study was described as "a new assessment by a new team". There was no new team. The same engineer signed both reports, and one of them had left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered with a private address in Portbury. Its sole managing director is Daniel Reyes, who was a member of the Port Authority Finance Committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we reached him by phone, he said he had nothing to add and ended the conversation.

A former tax official at the agency, who did not want to be named, said she had expressed her concerns three times in writing. “I was told this was approved from above and that I should process the bill,” she said. She never got a justification. Four months later, her job was dismantled, although the agency says the two events are not related.

The dredging itself has not been carried out so far. According to an investigation conducted in January for the port’s insurers, the sludge in the main canal is now 1.8 meters deeper than the safe limit. Two cargo ships were rejected this year. A third ship ran aground in February and had to wait eleven hours for the flood.

Local businesses say they pay the price. The fish market has lost about a fifth of its sales since 2021. A ferry company has suspended its summer service to the islands and the owner of the shipyard told us that he had laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The Authority receives £2.4 million in public funds annually. It has not published audited financial statements since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was launched last autumn, but did not provide any information on when a report will be available or whether the two payments are part of it.

In a written statement, the agency said it “takes its duties seriously” and that “all payments have been duly approved.” She did not answer our questions about why the reports are identical, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes filed a self-interest. She also refused to hand over the bills.

We asked the Ministry of Transportation if it knew about the payments before extending the agency’s subsidy. A spokesman said the ministry did not comment on individual cases. Local MP Hannah Whitlock has called for the board to resign.
