The Port Authority paid the same report twice.

The Port Authority of Westmer paid a consultancy £186,000 for a dredging research in March 2019 and then paid the same company £172,500 for a second study fourteen months later. Both reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 of the 48 pages matched, including a typo on page 12.

None of these payments were subject to tender. The administration's own rules require three written proposals for any contract valued in excess of £50,000. The administration's director general, Margaret Oduya, told us the rules were not broken because the work was "urgent and specialized."

However, this was not reported to the Board of Directors. The minutes of the meeting, held on May 9, 2020, indicate that the second study was described as a “new assessment conducted by a new team.” There was no such new team. Both reports are signed by the same two engineers, with one of them leaving the company before the second report was submitted.

The consultancy, Tidewell Associates, is registered at its home address in Portbury. Its sole director is Daniel Reyes, who served on the Port Authority Finance Committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two emails. When we contacted him by phone, he stated that he had no comment and interrupted the conversation.

A former administration finance official, who asked not to be identified, said she had written her concerns three times. “I was told that it was approved at the highest level and that I had to issue an invoice,” she said. She was never told why. Four months later, her position was abolished, though the administration claims the two events are unrelated.

The dredging itself has not yet been carried out. According to a study conducted in January for the port insurance company, the level of silt in the main fairway is now 1.8 meters below the safe level. This year, two cargo ships were turned away. The third ship ran aground in February and was forced to wait for tide for eleven hours.

Local businesses say they are paying for it. The seafood market has lost about a fifth of its revenue since 2021. The ferry operator cancelled summer service with the islands and the shipyard owner informed us that he had laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The administration receives £2.4 million in public funds each year. It has not published audited financial statements since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed an investigation was launched last fall, but did not say when his report would be released or whether the two payments were part of that investigation.

In a written statement, the administration said it "takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments were duly authorized." She did not respond to our questions about why the reports are the same, who approved the second payment and whether Mr. Reyes declared a conflict of interest. She also refused to provide copies of the accounts.

We asked the Department of Transportation whether the Department was aware of these payments before the administration's funding was extended. A ministry spokesman said the ministry did not comment on individual cases. A local MP, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the board to resign.
