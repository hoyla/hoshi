The Simer Port Authority paid two consultancy fees for the same report.

The Simer Port Authority paid Pound186 million to a consulting firm in March 2019 for a dredging study. Subsequently, 14 months later, £17.25 million was again paid to the same company for another study. Both reports are almost identical. By line-by-line, we find 41 pages of the 48-page report entirely consistent, including even a typing error on page 12.

On both occasions, no public tender was issued. The Port Authority itself requires three written offers for any contract in excess of £50,000. The chief executive officer of the Agency, Margaret Odua, told us that these provisions had not been violated, since the work was “very urgent and professional”.

However, the Board was not informed of this. The minutes of the 9 May 2020 meeting showed that the second study was described as “a reassessment by a new team”. In fact, no new teams have emerged. The two reports were signed by the same two engineers, one of whom left the company before the second report was delivered.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portbury. Its only director is Daniel Reyes, who served on the Finance Committee of the Port Authority until 2017. He did not reply to five e-mails and two letters we sent. When we contacted him by telephone, he said that “there was nothing to add” and that the call had ended.

A former finance official who refused to reveal her name stated that she had expressed doubts in writing on three occasions. “I was told that this had been approved by my superiors and that I should process the invoices,” she said. “She was never informed of the specific reasons. Four months later, her position was abolished, although the Port Authority indicated that there was no link between the two matters.

Dredging has not yet been carried out. According to a survey conducted in January for port insurance companies, the depth of sludge in the main channel has exceeded the safety limit of 1.8 metres. This year, two cargo ships have been denied access. The third vessel was stranded in February and had to wait 11 hours for the tide.

Local businesses have indicated that they are paying the price. Trading in fish markets has declined by about one fifth since 2021. A ferry operator cancelled the flight to the island in the summer, and the owner of the dock told us that he had laid off 6 of the 14 employees.

The Port Authority receives Pound2.4 million annually from public funds. Since 2020, the Bureau has not published audited financial reports. Audits in the region confirmed that an investigation had been initiated last fall, but did not disclose the timing of the investigation report and whether the two payments were included in the investigation.

In a written statement, the Port Authority stated that it “takes due care of its duties” and that “all payments are properly authorized”. It did not answer our questions about why the report was identical, who approved the second payment and whether Mr. Reyes had declared his interests. In addition, it refused to disclose the invoices.

We have asked the Ministry of Transport whether it is aware of these payments before renewing the allocation of the Port Authority. One speaker said that the Department did not comment on individual cases. Local Member of Parliament Hanna Whitlock called for the Board to resign.
