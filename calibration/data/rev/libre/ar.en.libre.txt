The port authority paid twice for the same report.

The Westmer Port Authority paid Pound186,000 to a consulting firm to conduct a survey in March 2019, and then paid to the same company Pound172,500 for a second study in fourteen months. The reports are very similar. We compared the two reports with a line and found that 41 of the 48 pages were identical, including a fission error on page 12.

None of the two instalments of competition have been put forward: its own rules of authority provide for three written presentations of any contract valued at more than £50,000. The executive head of the Authority, Margaret Odua, explained that the rules were not violated because the work was &quot; expedited and specialized &quot; .

This is not what the Governing Council was told.

The consulting firm, Tedwell Asuccitus, registered in a residential address in Portbri, the only member of its board, Daniel Reyes, who was a member of the Port Authority Finance Committee until 2017, did not respond to five e-mails and two letters, and when we spoke to him on the phone, he said he had nothing else to add and the call was terminated.

She said that she raised her concerns in writing three times, and she said, "I was told that it was approved by senior management and that I had to process the bill." She was not given any reason.

According to a survey conducted for port insurance companies in January, the sediment layer in the main corridor is 1.8 metres deeper than the safety limit, two shipping vessels were refused this year, and a third ship hit the platform in February and stopped for 11 hours waiting for the tide and islands.

Local companies say they pay the price.

The Authority receives Pound2.4 million annually as general funding. The Authority has not published audited accounts since 2020. The Regional Inspector confirmed that an investigation was opened last fall, but did not mention when the report was to be issued, or whether the payments were part of it.

In a written statement, the Authority stated that it &quot; takes its responsibilities seriously &quot; and that &quot; all payments were properly approved &quot; did not answer our questions as to why the reports were similar, who agreed to the second instalment, or whether Mr. Reyes had declared any conflicts of interest. It also refused to publish invoices.

We asked the Ministry of Transport if she knew about the payments before the power grant was renewed. A spokesman for the Ministry said that the Ministry did not comment on individual cases and invited a local parliament member, Hanna Witlock, to resign the Governing Council.
