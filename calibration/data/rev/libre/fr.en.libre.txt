The port authority has paid twice the same amount to a consulting firm for the same report.

The Westmere Port Authority paid Pound186,000 to a consulting firm for a dredging study in March 2019 and then paid the same amount of Pound172,500 to the same company fourteen months later for a second study. Both reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 of the 48 pages were identical, including a typing error on page 12.

Neither of the payments was tendered. The Authority's own rules require three written estimates for any contract exceeding £50,000. Its Executive Director, Margaret Oduya, told us that the rules had not been violated because the work was "urgent and specialized".

That is not what was said to the board of directors. The report of a meeting on 9 May 2020 indicates that the second study was described as "a new evaluation carried out by a new team". No new team existed. The same two engineers signed the two reports, and one of them left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portbury. Its only director is Daniel Reyes, who was a member of the finance committee of the port authority until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When contacted by telephone, he stated that he had nothing to add and hung up.

A former head of the authority's finances, who requested not to be appointed, stated that she had expressed her concerns in writing three times. "I was told that this was approved by management and that I had to process the invoice," she said. He's never been given a reason. His position was abolished four months later, although the authority claims that both events are unrelated.

Dredging work has not yet been completed. According to a study conducted for port insurers in January, sediments in the main channel are now 1.8 metres deeper than the safety limit. Two cargo vessels were refused this year. A third failed in February and had to wait eleven hours for the tide to rise.

Local companies say they pay the price. The fish market has lost about a fifth of its sales since 2021. A ferry operator cancelled his summer service to the islands, and the shipyard owner told us that he had fired six of his fourteen employees.

The Authority receives £2.4 million per year of public funding. It has not published audited accounts since 2020. The Regional Auditor confirmed that an investigation had been initiated last fall, but did not specify when it would be made public or whether the two payments were part of it.

In a written statement, the authority stated that it "takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments have been duly authorized". She did not answer our questions about why the reports were identical, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes declared a conflict of interest. She also refused to disclose the invoices.

We asked the Department of Transport if he was aware of these payments before renewing the authority's grant. A spokesperson stated that the department does not comment on individual cases. Local MP Hannah Whitlock called on Council members to resign.
