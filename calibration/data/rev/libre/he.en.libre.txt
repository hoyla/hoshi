The Port Authority paid the advisor a double amount for the same report.

Westmir’s Port Authority paid an 186,000-pound advisory firm for a study on the Valley of Ascension in March 2019, and then paid the same company a sum of 172,500 pounds for a second-century study. The two reports are almost identical. We compared them to a row and found that 41 of the 48 pages are identical, including the typing error on page 12.

None of the payments were announced to the tender. The internal rules of the PA require three written offers for each contract worth £50,000. CEO Margaret Odoya told us that the rules were not violated because the work was “a special and urgent.”

That’s not what the manager said. A meeting protocol held on May 9, 2020 indicates that the second study was described as a “renewal campaign by a new team.” There was no new team. Two identical engineers signed both reports, and one of them left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting company, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portbury. Her only director is Daniel Rice, who served on the Finance Committee until 2017. He did not answer five emails and two letters. When we arrived on the phone, he said he had nothing to add and silence the conversation.

A former financial official, who asked not to publish her name, said she raised written concerns three times. “I was told that it was blissful and that I had to process the invoice,” she said. She never got an explanation. Her job was cancelled four months later, although the PA claims the events are not related to each other.

The valley itself has not yet been performed. The mud layer on the main channel is now 1.8 meters across the safety border, according to a survey conducted for the port’s insurance companies in January. Two cargo ships were delayed this year. A third ship came up in February and had to wait 11 hours until the tide.

Local businesses say they pay the price. The fish market has lost about a fifth of its operations since 2021. A ferry operator cancelled his summer service to the islands, and the owner of the garage told us he was fired six of his fourteen employees.

The PA receives 2.4 million pounds annually in public funding. She has not released controlled financial reports since 2020. The visitor of the area confirmed that the investigation was opened last fall, but refused to say when it would be published, or whether both payments were included in it.

In a written statement, the PA said it was "deeply referring to its obligations" and that "all payments were approved properly." She did not answer our questions about the reason why the reports were identical, who authorized the second payment, or whether Mr. Rice declared a conflict of affairs. She also refused to release the accounts.

We asked the Ministry of Transport whether he was aware of the payments before he renewed the grant of the Authority. A spokesman for the office said the office does not respond to specific cases. The local parliament, here Whitlock, called the Executive Committee to resign.
