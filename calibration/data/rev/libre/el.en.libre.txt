The competent authority of the port paid a consulting firm twice for the same report.

The Westmere Harbour Authority paid £186,000 to a consulting company for a landing study in March 2019, and then paid the same company £172,500 for a second study, fourteen months later. The two reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 of the 48 pages are the same, including a printing error on page 12.

None of the payments went to competition. Its own regulations require three written tenders for any contract worth more than £50,000. Her principal, Margaret Oduya, told us that the regulations were not violated because the work was "emergency and skilled".

That was not what was said to the board. The minutes of a meeting held on 9 May 2020 indicate that the second study was described as "a new evaluation by a new group". There was no new team. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had left the company before the second report was delivered.

The consulting company, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portbury. Its only director is Daniel Reyes, who was a member of its financial committee of the port authority until 2017. She didn't answer five emails and two letters. When we contacted him by phone, he said he had nothing to add and interrupted the conversation.

A former official of the financial department of the authority, who asked not to be named, said she expressed her concerns in writing three times. "I was told that it had been approved by the upper tiers and that I had to process the invoice," he said. She was never given any reason. Her position was abolished four months later, although the authority states that the two events are not connected.

The landing itself has not yet been carried out. The amount of sediment on the main channel is now 1.8 meters deeper than the safe limit, according to a survey carried out on port insurers in January. Two cargo ships have been rejected this year. A third barricaded in February and had to wait eleven hours for the impenetrable.

Local businesses say they pay the price. The fish market has lost about a fifth of its revenue since 2021. A ferry company canceled its summer service to the islands, and the shipyard owner told us that he has fired six of his fourteen employees.

The authority receives £2.4 million annually in public funding. He has not published audited accounts since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was opened last fall, but did not mention when the report will be published, nor whether the two payments are part of it.

In a written statement, the authority stated that it "takes its obligations seriously into account" and that "all payments had been properly authorized." He did not answer our questions about why the reports are the same, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr Reyes declared a conflict of interest. He also refused to make the invoices public.

We asked the Ministry of Transport if he knew about the payments before renewing the authority's grant. A ministry representative said the ministry does not comment on individual cases. Local MP Hannah Whitlock has requested the resignation of the board.
