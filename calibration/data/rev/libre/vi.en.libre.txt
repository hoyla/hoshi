The port administration paid for a two-time consulting company for the same report.

Westmere Harbor Administration paid an 186,000-pound consulting company for a paper on the Stake in March 2019, and then paid that company $17,500 for a second study, 14 months later. These two reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 of the 48 pages were the same, including a misspelling on page 12.

Neither payment was made to bid. The rule of the administration itself required that there be three written-out papers for any contract worth over 50,000 pounds. The Executive Director of the Agency, Mrs Margaret Odoya, told us that the regulation was not violated because the work was "need and expertise".

However, that is not what the board is informed of. The minutes of a meeting took place on 9 May 2020 recorded that the second study was described as "a new rating by a new team". However, no new team existed. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them left the company before the second report was sent out.

The consulting company, Tidewell Associates, was registered at a home address in Portbury. The only person who held the directorship was Daniel Reyes, who had been a member of the finance committee of the port management until 2017. He did not answer five emails and two letters. When we contacted him over the phone, he said he had nothing to add and finished the call.

A former financial worker of the agency, who requested not to be named, stated that she had expressed her concerns in writing three times. "I was informed that it had been approved at higher levels and I should have handled the bill," she said. She was never explained why. Her work was cut down after four months, although the management said that the two events were unrelated.

The fact that the suit was still under way. According to a survey conducted for the port's insurance companies in January, the amount of sediment in the main channel is more than 1.8 meters in depth than the safety limit. Two freight ships were rejected this year. Another ship ran aground in February and had to wait until the tide came up.

Local businesses say they're paying the price. The fish market has lost about a fifth of its revenue since 2021. A ferry transport company cancelled the summer service to the islands, and owner of a shipyard said he had to fire six of the fourteen employees.

The administration received £4.4 million a year from the public budget. The agency has not yet released financial reports that have been audited since 2020. The regional inspector confirmed that an investigation had been opened last fall, but did not say when the report would be announced, or whether two payments were involved in the investigation.

In a written statement, the management said that they "responsibly carry out their obligations" and "all payments are properly authorized". The agency doesn't answer our questions about why the reports are the same, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes made a statement about his interests. The agency also refused to release the bills.

We asked the Ministry if we knew about these payments before it extended the grant to the management agency. A spokesman said the department did not comment on specific cases. The local representative of the parliament, Mrs Hannah Whitlock, called for the board to resign.
