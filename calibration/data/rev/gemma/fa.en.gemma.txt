The port authority paid double the amount for what appears to be the same report.

The Westmeir Port Authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 in March 2019 for a study related to dredging, and then paid the same firm £172,500 fourteen months later for another study. The two reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages were the same, including a typographical error on page 12.

Neither of these payments was made through a competitive bidding process. The organization's internal rules require obtaining three written proposals for any contract exceeding £50,000. Margaret Oduya, the CEO of the organization, told us that these rules were not violated because the work was "urgent and specialized."

This was not what the board was told. The minutes of a meeting held on May 9, 2020, show that the second study was described as a "re-evaluation by a new team." There was no new team. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, named "Tidwell Associates," is registered at a residential address in Portbury. The sole director of the company is Daniel Rees, who was a member of the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we called him by phone, he said he had nothing to add and hung up.

One former employee of the organization's finance department, who wished to remain anonymous, said that she had raised her concerns in writing on three occasions. She said, "I was told that this had been approved at a higher level and that I should process the invoice." She was not given any specific reasons. Four months later, her job was eliminated, although the organization claims that these two events are unrelated.

The dredging operation itself has not yet been carried out. According to a review conducted for the insurance companies covering the port, the amount of sediment in the main channel is now 1.8 meters deeper than the permitted level. This year, two cargo ships have been prevented from entering the port. Another ship ran aground in February and had to wait 11 hours for the tide to turn.

Local business owners say they are feeling the impact of this situation. The fish market has lost approximately a fifth of its business since 2021. One shipping company has cancelled its summer service to the islands, and the owner of a shipbuilding workshop told us that they have laid off six of their fourteen employees.

This organization receives £2.4 million annually from public funds. The organization has not published its audited financial statements since 2020. A regional inspector confirmed that an investigation was launched last autumn, but declined to comment on the report's findings or whether these two payments were related to it.

In a written statement, the organization stated that it "takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments have been properly authorized." The organization did not respond to our questions about why the reports are identical, who authorized the second payment, or whether Mr. Rees had declared any specific interests. They also declined to release the invoices.

We asked the Ministry of Transport whether they were aware of these payments before renewing the organization's funding. A spokesperson for the ministry said that the ministry does not comment on individual cases. The local Member of Parliament, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the resignation of the board of directors.
