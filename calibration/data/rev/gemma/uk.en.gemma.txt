The port authority paid consultants twice for the same report.

The Westmer Port Authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 for a dredging study in March 2019, and then paid the same firm another £172,500 for a second study just fourteen months later. Both reports were almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages were the same, including a typographical error on page 12.

Neither of these payments was made through a competitive bidding process. The authority's internal rules require obtaining three written proposals for any contract exceeding £50,000. The authority's CEO, Margaret Oduya, told us that these rules were not violated because the work was "urgent and specialized."

However, this was not what was presented to the board. The minutes of a meeting held on May 9, 2020, recorded that the second study was described as a "new assessment conducted by a new team." There was no new team. Both reports were signed by the same two engineers, and one of them had left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Porbury. Its sole director is Daniel Reyes, who was a member of the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we contacted him by phone, he said he had nothing to add and ended the conversation.

A former finance officer at the authority, who asked not to be named, said that she had raised her concerns in writing on three occasions. "I was told that it had been approved at a higher level, and that I should process the invoice," she said. She was never given any explanation. Four months later, her position was eliminated, although the authority claims that these two events are not related.

The dredging work has not yet been carried out. According to a survey conducted in January for the port's insurers, the silt in the main channel is currently 1.8 meters deeper than the safe limit. This year, two cargo ships have been turned away. Another ship ran aground in February and was forced to wait for the tide for eleven hours.

Local businesses say they are experiencing negative consequences. The fish market has lost nearly a fifth of its business since 2021. The ferry operator has cancelled its summer route to the islands, and the shipyard owner told us that he has laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The administration receives £2.4 million in government funding annually. It has not published audited financial reports since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was launched last autumn, but did not disclose when the report will be released, or whether these two payments are part of the investigation.

In a written statement, the administration said that it "takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." It did not respond to our questions about why the reports are identical, who authorized the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes declared a conflict of interest. It also refused to provide copies of the invoices.

We contacted the Department of Transport to find out if the ministry was aware of these payments before the administration's grant was extended. A spokesperson for the ministry said that the ministry does not comment on individual cases. The local Member of Parliament, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the board to resign.
