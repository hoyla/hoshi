The port authority paid a consulting firm twice for the same report.

The Westmere port authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 in March 2019 for a study on dredging, and paid the same firm £172,500 fourteen months later for a second study. The two reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages were identical, including a typographical error on page 12.

Neither of the payments was the result of a competitive bidding process. The authority's own rules require three written bids for any contract worth more than £50,000. The CEO, Margaret Oduya, told us that the rules were not violated because the work was "urgent and specialized."

However, this was not disclosed to the board. Minutes from a meeting on May 9, 2020, show that the second study was described as "a new assessment by a new team." There was no new team. The same engineer signed both reports, and one of them had left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a private address in Portbury. Its sole director is Daniel Reyes, who was a member of the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He has not responded to five emails and two letters. When we reached him by phone, he said he had nothing to add and ended the call.

A former finance officer at the authority, who wished to remain anonymous, said she had raised her concerns in writing three times. "I was told that this was approved from above and that I should process the invoice," she said. She never received an explanation. Four months later, her position was eliminated, although the authority claims that the two events were not related.

The dredging itself has not yet been carried out. According to an investigation conducted in January for the port's insurers, the silt in the main channel is now 1.8 meters deeper than the safe limit. Two cargo ships have been turned away this year. A third ship ran aground in February and had to wait eleven hours for the tide.

Local businesses say they are paying the price. The fish market has lost about a fifth of its revenue since 2021. A ferry company has suspended its summer service to the islands, and the owner of a shipyard told us that he has laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The authority receives £2.4 million in public funds each year. It has not published audited annual accounts since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was launched last autumn, but did not provide any details about when a report will be available or whether the two payments are part of it.

In a written statement, the agency said that it "takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments were properly approved." It did not answer our questions about why the reports are identical, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes had declared any conflicts of interest. It also refused to release the invoices.

We contacted the Ministry of Transportation to ask if it was aware of the payments before it extended the agency's grant. A spokesperson said that the ministry does not comment on individual cases. The local Member of Parliament, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the board to resign.
