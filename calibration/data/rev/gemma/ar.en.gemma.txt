The port authority paid consultancy fees twice for the same report.

The Port of Westmer paid a consultancy firm £186,000 to conduct a study on dredging operations in March 2019, and then paid the same firm £172,500 for a second study fourteen months later. The two reports were very similar. We compared the reports line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages were identical, including a spelling error on page 12.

Neither payment was put out to competitive tender. The authority's own rules state that three written bids must be obtained for any contract worth more than £50,000. The authority's CEO, Margaret Oduwa, explained that the rules were not broken because the work was "urgent and specialized."

This is not what was told to the board. Minutes from a meeting held on May 9, 2020, state that the second study was described as a "fresh assessment by a new team." There was no new team. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consultancy firm, "Tedwell Associates," is registered at a residential address in Portree. The sole director is Daniel Reyes, who was a member of the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we spoke to him by phone, he said he had nothing further to add and ended the call.

A former finance officer at the authority, who asked to remain anonymous, said that she raised her concerns in writing three times. She said, "I was told that it had been approved by senior management and that I should process the invoice." She was not given any explanation. Her job was eliminated four months later, although the authority says the two events are unrelated.

The dredging operations themselves have not yet been carried out. According to a survey conducted for the port's insurers in January, the sediment layer in the main channel is 1.8 meters deeper than the safe limit. Two cargo ships have been refused access this year. A third ship ran aground in February and was stuck for eleven hours waiting for the tide.

Local businesses say they are feeling the impact. The fish market has lost nearly a quarter of its trading volume since 2021. A ferry operator has cancelled its summer services to the islands, and the owner of a boatbuilding workshop told us he has laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The authority receives £2.4 million annually in public funding. The authority has not published audited accounts since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was opened last autumn, but did not mention when the report would be released, or whether the two payments were part of it.

In a written statement, the authority said that it "takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments were properly approved." It did not answer our questions about why the reports are similar, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reeves had declared any conflicts of interest. It also refused to publish the invoices.

We have asked the Department for Transport whether it was aware of the payments before the authority's grant was renewed. A spokesperson for the department said that the department does not comment on individual cases. The local Member of Parliament, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the board of directors to resign.
