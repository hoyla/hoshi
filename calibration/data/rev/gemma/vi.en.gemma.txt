The port authority paid a consulting firm twice for the same report.

The Westmere Port Authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 for a dredging study in March 2019, and then paid the same firm £172,500 for a second study, 14 months later. The two reports were almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages were the same, including a spelling error on page 12.

Both payments were not put out to tender. The authority's own regulations require three written quotes for any contract worth over £50,000. The authority's CEO, Margaret Oduya, told us that the regulations were not violated because the work was "essential and specialized."

However, that was not what the board was told. The minutes of a meeting held on May 9, 2020, recorded that the second study was described as "a new assessment by a new team." However, no new team existed. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portbury. The only person listed as a director is Daniel Reyes, who was a member of the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we contacted him by phone, he said he had nothing to add and ended the call.

A former finance employee of the authority, who asked not to be named, said she had raised her concerns in writing three times. "I was told it had been approved at a higher level and that I should process the invoices," she said. She was never given an explanation. Her position was cut after four months, although the authority said that the two events were not related.

The actual dredging work has not yet been carried out. According to a survey conducted for the port's insurance companies in January, the amount of sediment in the main channel is now 1.8 meters deeper than the safety limit. Two cargo ships have been refused access this year. Another ship ran aground in February and had to wait for 11 hours for the tide to come in.

Local businesses say they are feeling the impact. The fish market has lost about a fifth of its revenue since 2021. A ferry company has cancelled its summer service to the islands, and the owner of a shipyard said he has had to lay off six of his fourteen employees.

The regulatory body receives £2.4 million annually from the public purse. The organization has not published audited financial reports since 2020. A local investigator confirmed that an investigation was opened last autumn, but did not specify when the report would be released, or whether the two payments were related to the investigation.

In a written statement, the regulatory body stated that they "take their obligations seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." The organization did not respond to our questions about why the reports were identical, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes had declared any conflicts of interest. The organization also refused to release the invoices.

We asked the Department of Transport whether it was aware of these payments before it renewed the grant for the regulatory body. A spokesperson said that the department would not comment on specific cases. The local Member of Parliament, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the board to resign.
