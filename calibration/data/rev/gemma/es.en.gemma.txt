The port authority paid a consulting firm twice for the same report.

The Westmere Port Authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 for a dredging study in March 2019, and subsequently paid the same company £172,500 for a second study, fourteen months later. The two reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages were the same, including a typographical error on page 12.

Neither of the payments was subject to a public tender. The authority's own regulations require three written bids for any contract exceeding £50,000. Its CEO, Margaret Oduya, told us that the regulations were not violated because the work was "urgent and specialized."

That is not what was communicated to the board. The minutes of a meeting held on May 9, 2020, indicate that the second study was described as "a new assessment carried out by a new team." There was no new team. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portbury. Its sole director is Daniel Reyes, who was a member of the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we contacted him by phone, he said he had nothing to add and hung up.

A former finance official at the authority, who asked not to be named, said that she expressed her concerns in writing on three occasions. "I was told that it had been approved by management and that I should process the invoice," she said. She was never given a reason. Her position was eliminated four months later, although the authority claims that the two events are not related.

The dredging work itself has not yet been carried out. According to a study conducted for the port's insurers in January, the sediment in the main channel is now 1.8 meters deeper than is safe. Two cargo ships have been turned away this year. A third ran aground in February and had to wait eleven hours for the tide.

Local businesses claim they are paying the price. The fish market has lost approximately one-fifth of its business since 2021. A ferry operator canceled its summer service to the islands, and the owner of the shipyard told us that he has laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The authority receives £2.4 million annually in public funds. It has not published audited accounts since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was opened last autumn, but declined to say when the report would be published, or whether the two payments are part of it.

In a written statement, the authority stated that "it takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." It did not respond to our questions about why the reports are the same, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes declared a conflict of interest. It also refused to disclose the invoices.

We have asked the Ministry of Transport whether it was aware of the payments before renewing the authority's grant. A spokesperson said that the ministry does not comment on individual cases. The local MP, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the council to resign.
