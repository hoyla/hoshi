The port authority paid the same consulting firm twice for the same report.

The Westmere port authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 for a dredging study in March 2019, and then paid the same firm £172,500 fourteen months later for a second study. The two reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages were the same, including a typographical error on page 12.

Neither payment was the result of a competitive bidding process. The authority's own rules require three written bids for any contract exceeding £50,000. The CEO, Margaret Oduya, told us that the rules were not violated because the work was "urgent and specialized."

This is not what was told to the board of directors. The minutes of a meeting on May 9, 2020, state that the second study was described as "a new assessment carried out by a new team." No new team existed. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portbury. Its sole director is Daniel Reyes, who was a member of the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He has not responded to five emails and two letters. When contacted by phone, he said he had nothing to add and hung up.

A former finance officer at the authority, who asked not to be named, said she had raised her concerns in writing on three occasions. "I was told that it had been approved by management and that I had to process the invoice," she said. She was never given a reason. Her position was eliminated four months later, although the authority claims that the two events are not related.

The dredging work has still not been carried out. According to a study conducted for the port's insurers in January, the sediment in the main channel is now 1.8 meters deeper than the safety limit. Two cargo ships have been turned away this year. A third ran aground in February and had to wait eleven hours for the tide to come in.

Local businesses say they are paying the price. The fish market has lost approximately one-fifth of its revenue since 2021. A ferry operator has cancelled its summer service to the islands, and the owner of the shipyard told us that he has laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The authority receives £2.4 million in public funding each year. It has not published audited accounts since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was opened last autumn, but did not specify when it will be made public, or whether the two payments are part of it.

In a written statement, the authority stated that it "takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." It did not respond to our questions regarding the reasons for the identical reports, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes had declared a conflict of interest. It also refused to disclose the invoices.

We contacted the Ministry of Transport to inquire whether it was aware of these payments before renewing the authority's grant. A spokesperson stated that the ministry does not comment on individual cases. The local Member of Parliament, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the council members to resign.
