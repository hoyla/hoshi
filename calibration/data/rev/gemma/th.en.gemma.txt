The Port Authority paid a consulting firm twice for the same report.

The Westmeir Port Authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 for a study on dredging the navigation channel in March 2019, and later paid the same firm another £172,500 for a second study, just 14 months later. The two reports were remarkably similar. We compared the two reports line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages contained identical content, including a typographical error on page 12.

Both payments were made without a competitive bidding process, which, according to the authority's own regulations, requires at least three written bids for contracts exceeding £50,000. Margaret Oduya, the authority's CEO, stated that the regulations were not violated because the work was considered "urgent and specialized."

However, this was not disclosed to the board. Minutes from a meeting on May 9, 2020, indicated that the second study was described as a "re-evaluation by a new team," which was not the case. Two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portberrie. The sole director of the company is Daniel Reyes, who previously served on the Port Authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we contacted him by phone, he said there was nothing further to add and ended the conversation.

A former finance officer of the organization, who wished to remain anonymous, stated that she had raised her concerns in writing on three separate occasions. "I was told that the matter had been approved by senior management, and that I should proceed with issuing the invoices." She never received any explanation, and her position was eliminated four months later, although the organization claimed that the two events were unrelated.

The dredging of the navigation channel has not yet been carried out. Sediment accumulation in the main navigation channel is more than 1.8 meters deeper than the safety level, according to a survey conducted by the port's insurance company in January. Two cargo ships were denied access to the port this year, and a third ship ran aground in February, requiring it to wait for 11 hours for the tide to rise.

Local businesses have reported being negatively impacted by these actions. The fish market has lost approximately 20% of its revenue since 2021. Ferry operators have cancelled services to the islands during the summer months, and the owner of a shipyard told us that he has laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The organization receives £2.4 million in funding from the government each year. The organization has not published audited financial reports since 2020. A regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was launched last autumn, but did not specify when a report would be released, or whether the two payments were part of the investigation.

In a written statement, the agency stated that it "takes its responsibilities very seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." The agency did not respond to our questions regarding the reasons for the similarities between the two reports, who authorized the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes disclosed any potential conflicts of interest. Furthermore, the agency refused to release the invoices.

We contacted the Ministry of Transport to inquire whether they were aware of these payments. Before the ministry renewed the agency's funding, a spokesperson for the ministry stated that the ministry would not comment on specific cases. A local Member of Parliament, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the committee to resign.
