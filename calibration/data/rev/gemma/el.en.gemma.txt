The port authority paid a consulting firm twice for the same report.

The Westmere Port Authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 for a dredging study in March 2019, and then paid the same firm £172,500 for a second study, fourteen months later. The two reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages were the same, including a typographical error on page 12.

Neither payment was put out to tender. The authority's own regulations require three written bids for any contract worth over £50,000. Its director, Margaret Oduya, told us that the regulations were not violated because the work was "urgent and specialized."

This contradicts what was said to the board. The minutes of a meeting held on May 9, 2020, state that the second study was described as "a new assessment from a new team." There was no new team. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portbury. Its sole director is Daniel Reyes, who was a member of the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we contacted him by phone, he said he had nothing to add and ended the conversation.

A former employee of the authority's finance department, who asked not to be named, said that she had expressed her concerns in writing three times. "I was told that it had been approved by senior management and that I needed to process the invoice," she said. She was never given any explanation. Her position was eliminated four months later, although the authority claims that the two events are not related.

The dredging work has not yet been completed. According to a survey conducted for the port's insurers in January, the amount of sediment in the main channel is now 1.8 meters deeper than the safe limit. Two cargo ships have been rejected this year. A third ran aground in February and had to wait for eleven hours for the tide to recede.

Local businesses say they are paying the price. The fish market has lost approximately one-fifth of its revenue since 2021. One company that operates ferry services has canceled its summer service to the islands, and the owner of a local shipyard told us that he has laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The authority receives £2.4 million in public funding annually. It has not published audited accounts since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was opened last autumn, but did not say when the report would be published, nor whether the two payments are related to it.

In a written statement, the authority said that it "takes its obligations very seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." It did not respond to our questions about why the reports are identical, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes declared a conflict of interest. It also refused to disclose the invoices.

We asked the Department of Transport if it was aware of the payments before renewing the authority's grant. A spokesperson for the department said that the department does not comment on individual cases. The local MP, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the resignation of the board of directors.
