The harbor authority paid a consultancy firm twice for the same report.

In March 2019, the Westmere Harbor Authority paid a consultancy firm £186,000 for a drainage study, and then, fourteen months later, paid the same firm £172,500 for another study. Both reports are almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that out of 48 pages, 41 were the same, including a typographical error on page 12.

The tendering process was not followed for either payment. According to the authority's own rules, any contract exceeding £50,000 requires three written quotations. The authority's CEO, Margaret Oduya, told us that no rules were broken because the work was "urgent and specific."

The board was not informed. The minutes of a meeting held on May 9, 2020, state that the second study was described as "a new assessment carried out by a new team." No new team existed. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had already left the firm before the second report was released.

The consultancy firm, "Tidwell Associates," is registered at a residential address in Portbury. Its sole director, Daniel Reyes, was a member of the harbor authority's finance committee until 2017. He has not responded to five emails and two letters. When we contacted him by telephone, he said he had nothing to say and ended the conversation.

A former finance officer of the authority, who requested anonymity, stated that he had expressed concerns in writing on three separate occasions. "I was told that it had been approved at the highest level and that I should process the invoices," he said. He was never given any explanation. Four months later, his employment was terminated, although the authority claims that the two events are unrelated.

The dredging work has not yet begun. According to a survey conducted in January for the port's insurers, the sediment in the main channel is now 1.8 meters deeper than the safe limit. Two cargo ships have been turned back this year. A third ship ran aground in February and had to wait for eleven hours for the tide to come in.

Local businesses say they are paying the price. The fish market has lost approximately one-fifth of its business since 2021. A boat operator has cancelled his summer service to the islands, and the owner of the boatyard told us that he has laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The authority receives £2.4 million in public funds each year. It has not published audited accounts since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was launched last autumn, but did not disclose when it would report, or whether these two payments are part of the investigation.

In a written statement, the authority said that it "takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." They did not answer our questions about why the reports are identical, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes had declared any conflicts of interest. They also refused to release the invoices.

We have asked the Ministry of Transportation whether they were aware of these payments before the grant renewal. A spokesperson said that the ministry does not comment on individual cases. The local Member of Parliament, Hanna Whitelock, has called for the board to resign.
