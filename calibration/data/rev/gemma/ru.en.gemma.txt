The port authority twice paid the same consulting firm for the same report.

The Westmer Port Authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 for a study regarding dredging work in March 2019, and then paid the same firm £172,500 for a second study fourteen months later. Both reports were virtually identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages were the same, including a typographical error on page 12.

Neither of these payments was subject to a bidding process. The authority's own rules require obtaining three written bids for any contract exceeding £50,000. The authority's CEO, Margaret Oduya, told us that the rules were not violated because the work was "urgent and specialized."

However, this was not disclosed to the board of directors. The minutes of a meeting held on May 9, 2020, indicate that the second study was described as a "new assessment conducted by a new team." There was no such new team. Both reports were signed by the same two engineers, one of whom had left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portbury. Its sole director is Daniel Reyes, who was a member of the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we contacted him by phone, he stated that he had no comment and ended the call.

A former finance employee of the authority, who asked to remain anonymous, said that she had expressed her concerns in writing on three occasions. "I was told that it had been approved at the highest level, and that I should process the invoice," she said. She was never given an explanation for the payments. Four months later, her position was eliminated, although the authority claims that these two events are not related.

The dredging work has yet to be completed. According to a study conducted in January for the port's insurance company, the silt level in the main channel is currently 1.8 meters deeper than the safe level. This year, two cargo ships have been diverted. A third ship ran aground in February and was forced to wait for the tide for eleven hours.

Local businesses claim they are suffering as a result. The seafood market has lost approximately one-fifth of its revenue since 2021. The ferry operator has cancelled its summer service to the islands, and the owner of a shipyard has told us that he has laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The administration receives £2.4 million annually in government funding. It has not published audited financial reports since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was launched last autumn, but did not disclose when the report would be published, or whether these two payments are part of that investigation.

In a written statement, the administration stated that it "takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." It did not respond to our questions about why the reports are identical, who approved the second payment, and whether Mr. Reyes declared a conflict of interest. It also refused to provide copies of the invoices.

We contacted the Department of Transport to ask whether the department was aware of these payments before the administration's funding was extended. A spokesperson for the department stated that the department does not comment on individual cases. The local MP, Hannah Whitlock, has called for the resignation of the board of directors.
