The port authority paid the consultant double for the same report.

The Port Authority of Westmer paid a consulting firm £186,000 for a study on deepening channels in March 2019, and then paid the same firm £172,500 for a second study fourteen months later. The two reports were almost identical. We compared them line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages were the same, including a typographical error on page 12.

Neither of the payments was put out to tender. The port authority's internal rules require that three written bids be received for any contract exceeding £50,000. The CEO, Margaret Oduya, told us that the rules were not violated because the work was "urgent and special."

This is not what was told to the board of directors. Minutes from a meeting held on May 9, 2020, state that the second study was described as a "re-evaluation by a new team." There was no new team. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portbury. The sole director is Daniel Rice, who served on the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we reached him by phone, he said he had nothing to add and hung up.

A former finance officer at the port authority, who asked not to be named, said that she raised concerns in writing three times. "I was told that it had been approved by management and that I should process the invoice," she said. She never received an explanation. Her position was eliminated four months later, although the port authority claims that the events are unrelated.

The dredging of the channels themselves has not yet been carried out. A layer of silt in the main channel is now 1.8 meters deeper than the safety limit, according to a survey conducted for the port's insurance companies in January. Two cargo ships have been turned away this year. A third ship ran aground in February and had to wait 11 hours for the tide to come in.

Local businesses say they are paying the price. The fish market has lost nearly a quarter of its business since 2021. A ferry operator has cancelled its summer service to the islands, and the garage owner told us that he has laid off six of his fourteen employees.

The authority receives £2.4 million annually in public funding. It has not published audited financial reports since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was opened last autumn, but declined to say when it would be released, or whether the two payments are included in it.

In a written statement, the authority said that it "takes its obligations seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." It did not respond to our questions regarding the reason for the identical reports, who authorized the second payment, or whether Mr. Rice declared a conflict of interest. It also refused to release the invoices.

We asked the Department for Transport whether it was aware of the payments before renewing the authority's grant. A spokesperson for the department said that it does not comment on specific cases. The local Member of Parliament, Helena Whitlock, has called for the board of directors to resign.
