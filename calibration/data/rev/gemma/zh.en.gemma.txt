The Port of Seamer Authority paid consultancy fees twice for the same report.

In March 2019, the Port of Seamer Authority paid £186,000 to a consultancy firm for a dredging study. Subsequently, 14 months later, the authority paid the same firm another £172,500 for a separate study. The two reports were almost identical. A line-by-line comparison revealed that 41 out of 48 pages were completely consistent, including a typographical error on page 12.

Both payments were made without a public tender. The authority's own regulations require that any contract exceeding £50,000 must be accompanied by three written quotations. Margaret Oduya, the authority's CEO, told us that these regulations were not violated because the work was "urgent and required specialist expertise."

However, the board was not informed of this. The minutes of the board meeting on May 9, 2020, described the second study report as a "re-evaluation conducted by a new team." In reality, there was no new team. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had already left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consultancy firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portberrie. Its sole director is Daniel Reyes, who previously served on the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not respond to five emails and two letters sent to him. When contacted by phone, he stated, "There is nothing further to add," and ended the call.

A former finance officer, who wished to remain anonymous, said she had raised concerns about the payments on three separate occasions in writing. "I was told that it had been approved by a senior officer, and that I should process the invoices," she said. "She was never told the specific reasons." Four months later, her position was made redundant, although the port authority stated that there was no connection between the two events.

The dredging work has yet to be carried out. An investigation conducted for the port's insurance company in January revealed that the silt depth in the main channel had exceeded the safety limit of 1.8 meters. This year, two cargo ships have been denied access. A third cargo ship ran aground in February and had to wait 11 hours for the tide to turn.

Local businesses say they are paying the price. Since 2021, the fish market has seen a decline in trade of approximately one-fifth. A ferry operator has cancelled its summer service to the island, and the owner of the dock has told us that he has laid off six of his 14 employees.

The port authority receives £2.4 million annually from public funds. Since 2020, the authority has not published audited financial reports. The regional audit office confirmed that an investigation was launched last autumn, but did not disclose when the report would be released, or whether the two payments were included in the investigation.

In a written statement, the port authority said that it "takes its responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." It did not answer our questions about why the reports were the same, who authorized the second payment, and whether Mr. Reyes had declared any conflicts of interest. Furthermore, it refused to release the invoices.

We have asked the Department for Transport whether it was aware of these payments before renewing the port authority's funding. A spokesperson said that the department does not comment on individual cases. Local MP Hannah Whitlock has called for the board to resign.
