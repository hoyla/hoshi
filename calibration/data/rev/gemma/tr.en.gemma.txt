Port authorities made two payments to a consulting firm for the same report.

Westmere Port Authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 in March 2019 for a dredging operation, and then made a second payment of £172,500 to the same firm fourteen months later for a separate project. The two reports were almost identical. When we compared the reports line by line, we found that 41 out of 48 pages were the same, and even a typographical error on page 12 was present in both.

No tender process was followed for either payment. According to the authority's own rules, three written bids should be obtained for any contract exceeding £50,000. Margaret Oduya, the CEO of the authority, told us that these rules were not violated because the work was "urgent and required specialist expertise."

However, this was presented differently to the board. Minutes from a meeting held on May 9, 2020, described the second project as a "new assessment carried out by a new team." However, there was no evidence of such a new team. Both reports were signed by the same two engineers, and one of them had left the firm before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered to a residential address in Portbury. The sole director of the company is Daniel Reyes, who served on the port authority's finance committee until 2017. Mr. Reyes did not respond to five emails and two letters. When we contacted him by phone, he ended the conversation, saying he had nothing further to add.

A former finance officer at the port authority, who wished to remain anonymous, said that they had raised concerns about this issue in writing on three occasions. "I was told that it had been approved at a senior level and that the invoice needed to be processed," they said. No explanation was ever provided. Four months later, they were made redundant, but the authority stated that the two events were not related.

The dredging operation has still not been carried out. According to an investigation carried out for the port's insurance companies in January, the sediment buildup in the main channel is 1.8 meters deeper than the safe limit. Two cargo ships have been turned away this year. A third ran aground in February and had to wait for eleven hours for the tide to rise.

Local businesses say they are paying the price. Sales at the fish market have fallen by approximately a third since 2021. A ferry operator has cancelled summer services between the islands, and the owner of a boat repair workshop has been forced to lay off six of his fourteen employees.

The port authority receives £2.4 million in public funds annually. They have not published audited financial statements since 2020. The regional auditor confirmed that an investigation was launched last autumn, but did not specify when the investigation would be concluded or whether the two payments were part of the investigation.

In a written statement, port officials stated that they "take their responsibilities seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." They did not respond to our questions regarding why the reports were identical, who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Reyes had declared any conflicts of interest. They also refused to release the invoices.

We contacted the Ministry of Transportation to inquire whether the officials were aware of these payments before renewing the grant. A spokesperson for the ministry stated that the ministry does not comment on individual cases. Local MP Hannah Whitlock has called for the resignation of the board of directors.
