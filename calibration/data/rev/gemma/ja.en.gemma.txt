The Westmeath Port Authority made two payments to the same consulting firm for what appears to be the same investigative report.

In March 2019, the Westmeath Port Authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 for a dredging survey. Fourteen months later, the same company was paid £172,500 for a separate investigation. A detailed comparison of the two reports revealed that 41 out of 48 pages were identical, and 12 pages contained the same typographical errors.

These payments were not made through a formal bidding process. According to the Port Authority's regulations, contracts exceeding £50,000 require three written bids. The Chief Executive Officer, Margaret Oduya, explained that this regulation was not violated because the work was considered "urgent and specialized."

However, a different explanation was provided to the board. The minutes of a meeting held on May 9, 2020, stated that the second investigation was a "new assessment by a new team." However, no such new team existed. Both reports were signed by the same two technicians, and one of them had already left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidewell Associates, is registered at a residential address in Portberry. The sole director is Daniel Legge, who served on the Port Authority's finance committee until 2017. He has not responded to five emails and two letters, and when contacted by phone, he stated that he "could not comment" and ended the call.

A former finance officer at the Port Authority (whose name has not been disclosed) stated that she had expressed her concerns in writing on three occasions. "I was told that it had been approved by senior management and instructed to process the invoices," she said. She was not given any explanation for the situation. Four months later, she was dismissed, although the Port Authority claims that these two events were unrelated.

To date, no dredging work has been carried out. An investigation conducted by the Port Authority's insurance company in January found that the depth of sediment in the main waterways was 1.8 meters deeper than the safety standard. Since the beginning of the year, two cargo ships have been refused entry. Furthermore, three ships have run aground, one of which had to wait for 11 hours for high tide.

Local businesses are reporting negative impacts. The fish market has lost approximately one-fifth of its trade since 2021. One ferry company has suspended its summer service to the island, and the owner of a shipyard has laid off six of his 14 employees.

The Port Authority receives £2.4 million in public funds annually. Since 2020, it has not published audited financial reports. The regional audit agency confirmed that an investigation was launched last autumn, but did not specify when the results would be released, nor whether the two payments in question were included in the scope of the investigation.

In a written statement, the Port Authority stated that it "takes its obligations seriously" and that "all payments were properly authorized." It did not respond to our questions regarding the reason for the identical reports, the person who approved the second payment, or whether Mr. Legge declared a conflict of interest. It also refused to disclose the invoices.

We inquired with the Department of Transport whether it was aware of these payments before the Port Authority's grant was renewed. A spokesperson for the department stated that "the ministry does not comment on individual cases." The local Member of Parliament, Hana Whitlock, is calling for the resignation of the board.
