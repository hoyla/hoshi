Westmore Port Authority Paid Consulting Firm Twice for Identical Reports

The Westmore Port Authority paid a consulting firm £186,000 in March 2019 for a sediment removal study, and then paid the same firm another £172,500 14 months later for a similar study. The two reports were almost identical. We compared the two reports line by line and found that 41 out of 48 pages were the same, and 12 pages contained typos.

Neither contract went through a bidding process. According to the port authority's own regulations, contracts exceeding £50,000 require three written quotes. CEO Margaret Oduya explained that the regulations were not violated because the work was "urgent and specialized."

However, the board of directors was presented with a different narrative. The minutes of a meeting held on May 9, 2020, described the second study as a "new assessment by a new team." However, there was no new team. The same two engineers signed both reports, and one of them had left the company before the second report was submitted.

The consulting firm, Tidwell Associates, is registered to a residential address in Portbury. The sole board member is Daniel Reyes, who served on the port authority's finance committee until 2017. He did not respond to our five emails and two letters. When we contacted him by phone, he said, "I have nothing further to add" and ended the call.

A former finance officer at the port authority, who requested anonymity, said she had raised concerns about the matter in writing on three separate occasions. "I was told it was approved at the top, and I had to process the invoices," she said. She never received an explanation for the decisions. Four months later, her position was eliminated, but the port authority claims that the two events are unrelated.

In reality, the sediment removal work has not yet been carried out. An investigation conducted by the port's insurance company in January found that the depth of sediment in the main channel is 1.8 meters deeper than the safety limit. This year alone, two cargo ships have been denied access. Another cargo ship ran aground in February and had to wait 11 hours for the tide to turn.

Local businesses are suffering as a result. The fish market has seen a decrease of approximately 20% in trade since 2021. One ferry company has suspended its summer service to the island, and the owner of a boat repair shop has laid off six of his 14 employees.

The port authority receives £2.4 million in public funds annually. They have not released audited financial reports since 2020. The local auditor began an investigation last year, but has not disclosed when the results will be released, nor whether the two payments in question are included in the scope of the investigation.

In a written statement, the port authority stated that "it takes its obligations seriously, and that all payments were properly approved." They declined to answer questions about the reason for the report, the person who authorized the second payment, and whether Daniel Reyes reported a conflict of interest. They also refused to release the invoices.

We inquired with the Department of Transportation whether they were aware of these payments, and whether they were aware before renewing the port authority's funding. A spokesperson stated that the department does not comment on individual cases. Local councilor Hannah Whitlock has called for the board to resign.
